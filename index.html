Important: This is a mathematical calculator, not trading or investment advice. Fees, funding, slippage, liquidation mechanics and exchange-specific contract rules can change the actual result.

How to calculate position size

  1. Enter your account balance.
  2. Choose the maximum percentage of the account you are willing to lose if the stop is hit.
  3. Enter your entry and stop-loss prices.
  4. Use the calculated position size instead of changing size because a setup feels better.

For a simple linear crypto position: risk amount = account balance × risk %, then position size = risk amount ÷ |entry − stop|.

FAQ

Does leverage change my maximum loss?

Not by itself. If the position is sized so the stop represents your chosen risk, leverage mainly changes the margin required. Real exchange mechanics can introduce liquidation, fees, funding and slippage.

Can I use this for BTC and other crypto?

Yes for simple linear price-based sizing where one unit of the asset gains or loses approximately the same amount as its price movement. Exchange-specific contract sizes may require a different calculation.

What if my stop is on the wrong side of my entry?

The calculator uses the absolute distance between entry and stop. For a long trade, the stop is normally below entry; for a short trade, it is normally above entry.

Does this include trading fees?

No. Fees, funding, spread and slippage are not included in the basic result.
